Built for No One

The Rise and Ruin of the E-topian Dream

Smart cities keep failing, and not because the code is bad. They fail because nobody living on the land was ever in a position to say no.

The book follows sixteen cities built from scratch on five continents, including NEOM in Saudi Arabia, Songdo in South Korea, Forest City in Malaysia, Xiongan in China, California Forever and Sidewalk Toronto. Most had deep pockets, famous designers and a founder with a vision. Almost none delivered. The book explains why, and gives readers a framework for spotting the same warning signs in the projects being pitched today.

I was finishing my PhD in Toronto when Sidewalk Labs announced its plan for a data-driven neighborhood on the city’s waterfront. The project ended in 2020. This book grew out of watching it unravel.

About

A new industry arrives in a city before anyone has written the rules for it. Over my career I have studied that moment with fracking in Appalachia, Uber and Lyft, food delivery apps, sidewalk delivery robots, billion-dollar smart city megaprojects, and now artificial intelligence. The technologies differ. The pattern repeats. A company shows up with a product, residents feel the effects first, and city hall is left to respond with tools built for a different era.

My research asks how cities make those decisions, why some adapt well, and why others are left cleaning up afterward. I draw on interviews with officials, policy documents, surveys of local politicians, and comparative datasets covering more than a hundred cities worldwide.

I am an Associate Teaching Professor at Syracuse University’s Maxwell School of Citizenship and Public Affairs, where I direct the online Bachelor of Professional Studies in Policy Studies. Many of my students are working adults finishing a degree while holding down a job, and they keep my work grounded. I hold a PhD in Planning from the University of Toronto and a Master of Public Administration from Cornell. I have published more than thirty articles, chapters and reports in outlets including Urban Affairs Review, the Journal of the American Planning Association, Cities and Planning Theory & Practice, and I serve as Area Editor for Digital and Data-Driven Transformations in Governance at Data & Policy.

I live in Vancouver, a city that argues about housing the way other cities argue about sports. I am also a Buffalo Bills fan, so I know something about watching big plans fall apart.
